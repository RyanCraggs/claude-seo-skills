# House voice — weekly SEO report

The report sounds like a skeptical analyst who respects the reader's time, not a dashboard doing a victory lap. Match the tone of the sample reports below.

## Principles

1. **Lead with the real story, not the flattering number.** If clicks rose but impressions fell, the headline is the tension, not the click gain. ("Decent week sitewide, but almost all of it is the homepage, and the click gain came alongside fewer impressions — worth a look rather than a victory lap.")
2. **Flag, don't over-explain.** GSC totals can't tell you *why* position or query-mix shifted. Say so and stop. ("Clicks up while impressions drop usually means a position or query-mix shift — I can't confirm which from GSC totals alone. Flagging it, not explaining it.")
3. **Name small samples as noise, not trends.** Single/low-digit clicks are not a signal. ("outside the homepage, every path is in single-digit clicks. Those percentages are small-sample noise, not trends.")
4. **Separate signal from noise per metric.** For low-CTR content, impressions are the leading signal and tiny click moves are noise. ("Impressions are the real signal here; the click move is noise.")
5. **Frame the blog as the leading indicator (on a young blog, impressions are the signal; blog clicks are single digits).** It's where new content lands, so blog movement is the early read on content strategy. ("This is the bucket we're watching as the leading indicator... a second week like this would be a real signal.")
6. **Be concrete.** Always "X vs Y (−Z%)", never "down significantly". Round large numbers (22.6k, 609k); keep small ones exact.
7. **Call out new surfaces.** If a section/page appears from ~nothing, note it and say to confirm what launched. ("New section detected: /shop — 0 clicks last week, 76 clicks / 2.7k impressions this week. Worth confirming what launched.")
8. **No hype adjectives, no filler.** Cut "robust", "strong", "impressive". Let the numbers carry it.
9. **End with a scope caveat** — what's filtered, what the data can't say, sample-size limits.
10. **Don't invent causes or AI-visibility data.** AI Overview / ChatGPT / Perplexity / DR / search volume are not in GSC. Leave them for the (future) Gumshoe/Semrush layer; never fabricate them.

## Structure

```
{Site} — Weekly SEO Report ({Mon D – Mon D, YYYY})

{One–two sentence lead: the real story, honest about tension.}

* Sitewide: {clicks} clicks ({+/-%} WoW), {impressions} impressions ({+/-%})
* Blog (/blog/): {clicks} ({+/-%}), {impressions} ({+/-%}) — {how it relates to sitewide / leading-indicator framing}
* {Optional extra buckets: core funnel, etc.}
* {New surfaces / newly published pages}

Highlights:
* {Winning page} — {what moved: clicks delta, and if pulled, a keyword position move like 23 -> 11}
* {Newly published page + date if known}

Caveat: {scope of the filter, sample-size limits, what GSC can't explain}
```

Keep it tight — the samples are ~8–12 bullets total, not a wall of text.

## Reference samples (the target)

Both samples use a fictional brand (Northwind, a sleep-supplement site) and synthetic numbers. The
voice and structure are the point.

### Northwind — Weekly SEO Report (Sep 30 – Oct 6, 2026) — the current target format

> **Northwind — Weekly SEO Report (Sep 30 – Oct 6, 2026)**
> _Compared against Sep 23–29, 2026 (both windows run Wed–Tue, per the requested dates)_
>
> Clicks fell by more than half, but this is a branded-demand story, not a /blog/ or ranking story. The prior week had a branded search spike on Sep 26–28 (141 clicks on Sep 27 alone vs a ~27/day baseline). "northwind" went from 142 to 44 clicks at the same average position (1.4), and the homepage absorbed almost the entire click loss. GSC can't say what drove the spike, but it looks like a news or PR bump fading rather than lost rankings. /blog/ impressions halved too, but on a small base.
>
> • **Sitewide:** 197 clicks (−52.9% WoW, from 418) and 3,051 impressions (−26.1%, from 4,126)
> • **Blog (/blog/, all locales):** 3 clicks (flat, from 3) and 436 impressions (−52.3%, from 914). That's about 44% of the sitewide impression drop. Impressions are the signal here, since clicks are too small to read. The prior week's /blog/ impressions were themselves inflated by a Sep 24–27 bump (104–221/day vs ~45–95/day this week).
> • **Core (non-blog):** 194 clicks (−53.3%, from 415) and 2,615 impressions (−18.6%, from 3,212)
> • **Homepage (https + http):** 108 clicks (−66.4%, from 321). That's about 96% of the sitewide click loss.
>
> **Top movers by clicks (Blog)**
> • None worth listing. Every /blog/ URL had 0–1 clicks in both weeks, which is noise.
>
> **Top movers by impressions (Blog)**
> • /blog/magnesium-glycinate-vs-citrate: **−244 impressions** (262 → 18), avg position 5.1 → 7.0, clicks 1 → 0. GSC shows almost no query detail for this page (anonymized long-tail), so I can't tie it to a keyword.
> • /blog/melatonin-dosage-guide: **−219 impressions** (252 → 33), avg position 5.4 → 7.2
> • /blog/magnesium-before-bed (new, all locales): **+141 impressions** (0 → 141). EN 38, DE 44, ES 39, PT 15, FR 5, and 0 clicks so far.
>
> **Highlights**
> • **New page:** /blog/magnesium-before-bed. It had no impressions in Sep 16–29 (I checked; only an older /news/ article on the same phrase matched), so this is a genuine first appearance. It's already showing up in 5 locale versions at positions ~5–8.
> • **Slipped:** the two English supplement explainers (magnesium-glycinate-vs-citrate, melatonin-dosage-guide) each lost ~87–93% of impressions and about 2 positions. Last week's numbers were boosted, so one more week will tell us whether this is a real decline or a return to baseline.
> • **Steady:** /blog/how-much-deep-sleep held at 112 impressions (from 131) and picked up the section's only English click.
>
> _Caveat: These numbers cover northwind.example pages with /blog/ in the URL, including the /es/, /fr/, /de/ and /pt/ mirrors. data_state=all. Blog clicks are in single digits, so the percentage changes there are noise; impressions are the usable signal. GSC can't explain why branded demand spiked, and AI-visibility isn't included._

What makes this sample work:
- The lead names where the sitewide move actually came from (branded demand on the homepage), so the reader doesn't blame the content program.
- It names a spike with its baseline instead of just reporting the % change.
- It says "none worth listing" for click movers instead of padding with 1-click noise.
- New pages are verified, and locale mirrors are grouped into one line with a breakdown.
- A "watch this" claim (the two explainers) sets up next week's follow-up.
